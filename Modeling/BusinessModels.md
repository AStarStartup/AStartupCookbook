A business model is the description of how a company creates, captures, and delivers value, stated in parts that can be checked separately. The working form keeps the parts distinct: who the customer is, what the job is, what the value is, how it is paid for, what it costs, and what keeps it from being copied.

## Business Model Canvas

The canvas is the one-page layout of those parts. It is a working draft, not a deliverable: fill it from what you know, mark the assumptions, and test the one part that is most likely to be wrong before you invest in the rest. Do not treat a completed canvas as evidence that the model works; it is a map of the claims to check.

## Pricing Models

Pricing is the part of the model the founder controls most directly and gets wrong most often. The working set: a price the customer will pay for the job, a cost structure the price covers, and a margin that survives the founder's own time. Route the tax and contract treatment to the appropriate specialist. Do not set the price from a competitor; set it from the value and the cost, then compare.

## Tax Models

The tax model is the record of what the business owes, when, and to whom, stated in the terms the founder actually files under. This is the part that must come from the appropriate specialist, not from this page. The page names the question; the specialist gives the answer.

## Market Defense Models

Market defense is the answer to the question: what stops a competitor from copying what you have and taking the customer? The honest answers are the things that take time or money to replicate: a switching cost, a network effect, a distribution you own, or a regulatory position. If the answer is "nothing, they would have to be as good as us," that is not a defense and the model should say so.

## Pivot Models

A pivot is the record of a decision to change one part of the model because the evidence said the current part was wrong. It is not a failure of the whole; it is a correction to a specific claim. The working form names the claim that was wrong, the evidence, the part that changes, and the parts that stay. Do not pivot the whole model on one failed experiment; correct the specific part the evidence actually addressed.
