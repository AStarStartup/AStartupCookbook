### Time Management

Time management for a solo founder is not about squeezing more hours into the day. It is about allocating the hours you have to the tasks that move the company forward, and protecting those hours from the tasks that feel urgent but do not move the company.

The three rules:

1. **The highest-leverage task gets the first block of the day.** The first block is your peak attention. You do not check email, you do not answer Slack, you do not triage the kanban board. You do the one task that, if you do nothing else today, still moves the company forward. That task gets the first block.
2. **Batch the low-leverage tasks.** Email, Slack, admin, meetings. These tasks are not done one at a time; they are done in a batch. Two or three times a day, you check in, you process the batch, and you close the tab. The rest of the day, the email is not your problem.
3. **The day ends with the next day's first task.** Before you leave, you write down the one task you will do first thing tomorrow. This eliminates the morning decision, which is an attention drain. You sit down and do the thing you already decided to do.

The solo founder does not have a calendar with meetings. The solo founder has a calendar with blocks. The blocks are the tasks. The tasks are the company. The company is the product. The product is the answer to the customer's problem. Time management is the allocation of attention to the answer.
