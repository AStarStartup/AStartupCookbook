### Temporal Learning

This chapter uses temporal learning to mean scheduling practice over time. Distinguish remembering a concept from being able to perform the task safely.

#### Evidence and limits

Dunlosky and colleagues' review rated practice testing and distributed practice as high-utility learning techniques across the conditions they reviewed.[9] It rated interleaved practice as moderate utility with a more limited evidence base.[9] The review concerns learning techniques; it does not prove that alternating unrelated startup projects improves a founder's productivity.

Do not use a universal claim that people forget 70 percent in a day or that one review "resets" a curve. Retention depends on the material, learner, practice, delay, and test.

#### A practice plan

1. Choose a skill and a checkable task, such as explaining a pricing formula or configuring a small test service.
2. Learn from a primary reference. Write a short explanation and try the task.
3. After a delay, attempt retrieval or the task without looking at the answer. Compare with the reference and correct errors.
4. Revisit it later in a different, relevant example. Increase or decrease the delay according to performance and the date when the skill is needed.
5. Test transfer to a real task with appropriate review. Familiarity with documentation is not acceptance evidence.

A schedule such as day 1, day 3, day 7, and day 30 is a starting experiment, not an optimal interval prescription. Record attempts, errors, and corrective feedback. Choose study times around your energy and responsibilities; this chapter does not establish a universally best time of day.

#### Interleaving without constant switching

Practice related problem types that require deciding which method applies. For example, compare gross-margin and cash-runway problems after learning each. Do not translate this into mandatory hourly switches between marketing and debugging. Protect work that needs continuity.

#### A local model as a practice aid

Use [Local LLM Tasks](../Dev/LocalLLM.md) to draft questions from a trusted reference. Require a source location for each answer. Attempt the questions before viewing the answers, then check against the primary material or an executable test. The model's answer is not an authoritative answer key.

Store the task, attempts, and review dates outside the chat. Ordinary inference does not teach the model permanently; the learning goal here is the human's skill.
