### Attention

Attention is the selection and maintenance of focus. For a founder, the practical question is which interruptions prevent useful work and which are necessary for customers or safety.

#### What the evidence supports

Sophie Leroy's 2009 paper studied transitions between work tasks in two experiments. Its abstract reports difficulty disengaging from unfinished tasks and poorer subsequent performance.[14] That does not establish a universal recovery time, a fixed number of productive hours per day, or a rule that every interruption destroys flow.

Use the research as a reason to examine task transitions, not as a stopwatch. The full study's methods and limits matter before generalizing to your team.

#### A small operating experiment

1. Select one recurring task and define a useful output: an interview record, verified fix, or completed reconciliation.
2. Record a baseline over comparable work blocks: duration, interruptions, completed output, errors, and required response times. Do not record private message contents.
3. Try one change, such as batching routine notifications or writing a restart note before switching tasks.
4. Compare equivalent blocks. Note differences in difficulty, sleep, urgent incidents, and other confounders.
5. Retain the change only if output or accuracy improves without harming customer support or wellbeing.

These are proposed management practices, not clinically validated treatment or guaranteed productivity gains. Adjust block length to the person and task. Some founders' best hours are not in the morning.

#### Product design

Make the primary task and progress visible. Use meaningful notification controls and progressive disclosure without hiding prices, risks, or other information users need. Measure task success and unnecessary interruptions rather than time spent in the product.

See [UX Principles](../Design/UXPrinciples.md). Do not assume that fewer buttons or more engagement automatically makes a product better.

#### Agents are different

A model's technical attention mechanism is not the same process as human attention. Use [Context Window Engineering](../Productivity/ContextWindowEngineering.md) for token budgets, truncation, and retrieval. Saving a rule to a file helps only when the runner reads it into the current context.

For your own work, leave a short handoff before switching: what is verified, what remains uncertain, and the next concrete action. Test whether this helps rather than calling it a universal reset.
