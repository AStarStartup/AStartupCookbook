A marketing presentation is a short argument for a specific decision, not a tour of everything the product does. It leads with the audience's problem, states the claim, gives the evidence, names the risk, and ends with the one thing being asked for.

## Sales Tactics

The tactics that hold up are the ones that do not require the audience to distrust the presenter: lead with their problem in their words, ask for a small reversible commitment before the large one, and name the risk openly rather than letting them discover it. Anything that only works because the audience does not know better will come back as churn or a refund.

## Pitfalls

The standing failures are the feature tour in place of the argument, the invented customer quote, the price revealed only at the end, and the deck that is a slide show instead of a decision. Each one costs the trust the presentation is trying to buy.
