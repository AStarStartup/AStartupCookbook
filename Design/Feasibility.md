Feasibility asks whether a design can be made to work within the constraints you actually have: cost, time, parts, skills, and regulation. Judge it before you invest in detail, and separate the questions.

## Feasibility

Ask each dimension in turn and record the answer and its evidence:

* Technical: can it be built with known methods and available parts or tooling? Which part is unproven?
* Financial: does it fit the budget, and what is the cheapest way to test the risky part first?
* Operational: can it be made, shipped, serviced, and supported by the people you have?
* Regulatory and safety: what must be true before it may be sold or used? Route the legal answer to the appropriate specialist.

Record the specific constraint that would kill the design, and the cheapest experiment that would expose it.

## Design Metrics

Choose the few numbers that decide the design: a cost per unit, a target dimension, a failure rate, a cycle time, a power or weight budget. For each, state the target, the unit, and how it will be measured. A metric without a unit and a measurement method is an opinion, not a metric.

## Decision Tradeoff Matrix

When several designs are live, lay them in a table: each design as a row, each weighted metric as a column, and the constraint that rules the decision named separately. Do not average the row when a single hard constraint already disqualifies a design. Record which design was chosen, why, and what would reverse the choice.
