Pen and paper is the cheapest design tool: it is fast, reversible, and shares no version. Use it before you open a design tool, and keep the page dated and titled so you can find the idea again.

## Concept Table

A concept table is a grid of options. Put the things you are choosing between down the left, the things you are choosing by across the top, and mark each cell with the option, a check, or a known problem. It keeps the choices visible at once and forces you to see the combinations you would otherwise skip. Fill the cells with what you know and a question mark for what you have not checked.

## Concept Fan

A concept fan spreads one central idea outward into a large number of variations. Start with the core requirement in the center, branch out one constraint or feature at a time, and let the branches diverge widely before you judge them. The point is volume and range, not quality: most branches die, and the useful one is often the uncomfortable one. Judge the fan only after it is finished, so early pruning does not hide the idea you needed.
